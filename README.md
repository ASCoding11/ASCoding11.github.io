Physics is a fixed 120 Hz step with the player as an upright cylinder; moving parts are placed by time alone, so every player who shares the round clock sees the same tower. A jump reaches 3.37 (3.67 counting the pop onto a ledge just above your feet) and you run at 7.5. In the air the stick only nudges you (`airControl`): measured against the way you're flying, it speeds you up (`AIR_ACC`, but never past your take-off speed, or the walk speed if that was slower), turns you (`AIR_TURN`) and slows you (`AIR_BRAKE`), so holding back stops a running jump instead of turning you round, and holding sideways for a whole jump moves you about 2.3; from a standing jump the stick gets the full `AIR_ACC`. Sections keep the step from one route part to the one after next above 3.67 (a big jump can't skip a part), keep beams longer than a running jump, and make hazards too tall or too fast to simply hop. When changing a section, also try running jumps (with the real `simStep`) from each part of its route to every later one, to make sure none can be skipped. Each section in `MODS` is built in its own frame: it starts on the 4 × 4 pad centred at (0, 0, 8) and must end with `B.exit(H)`, its exit pad at (0, H, -8), staying inside radius 12.5 and above y = 0. Towers turn every other section 180° and may mirror any, and between sections there is a porch, a truss and a landing 5.5 higher (`RISE`), more than a jump plus a body (5.37), so nothing you can stand on in one section lets you reach the next one's low parts. Every section also lists a route for the bot (`B.j`, `B.w`, `B.jon`): before each jump the bot simulates it with the real `simStep` and only jumps when it lands (in the air it asks for just the speed that brings it down over the target, `aimAir`, as a player who commits to a jump would), and it waits or hops past hazards, so a route that completes proves the section can be beaten with the real movement numbers. When adding or changing a section, run the bot over it at several difficulties, mirrored and not, and at several start times, and over whole towers (`verifyModule`, `runBot`; the page exposes them as `window.__tw` in automated browsers). Online, like Chromaflage, everything travels through each player's own node at `rooms/tw-<code>/players/<uid>`, which must always hold `n, c, x, z, h, ts`: position and animation (`y`, `a`), the round the player is in (`r`) and a finish claim (`f`, `round:milliseconds`). Other players check the round before using it (`cleanRS`: finite times within a day of now, a finish list of `[uid, ms, name]`, names cleaned), so a broken host can't freeze their page. The host takes finish claims at their word, so it isn't cheat-proof. If the host leaves, the next player carries on from the last round it saw. Nothing about a round is kept online after it ends. The choice is saved as `spire:view`. It shows total time, players (people who opened a game, apart from those who only opened the lobby pages), sessions, how many people have a name, time by game as a bar list (Back Room games by their folder labels, plus the archived games, and the Back Room and phone pages counted as lobby), and time per person (one row per named account, all its browsers together, or per unnamed browser as "Visitor" plus the end of its ID), sortable, searchable, with a per-game breakdown on tap. Filters: today, 7 days, 30 days or all time (each a date-bounded query), game, and device. It loads at most the newest 10,000 page visits for the range, counting every page on the site because the labels are filtered in the browser, and says so when it hits that cap. Time spent in a game opened from the home page's reveal counts under the game; the home page itself (`home`) isn't counted as Back Room time.
  - **Chat**, the safety log in Firestore `chatlog` (written by `social.js`): cabin-chat messages and reports, newest first, opened with the admin key, filterable by kind, person (a report also matches the person it's about), words and dates (at most 2,000 per load, and it says when it hits that). Entries that won't open (written by something else, or sealed to a different key) are counted and shown as such. There's no delete: the rules don't allow it (old entries can be deleted in the Firebase console).
  - **Feedback**, the `feedback` notes as a to-do list (the newest 500): open ones first, newest first; click a note or its checkbox to mark it done or open again (sets `done`); filter by game and kind; "Show done" shows the done ones after the open ones. "Hide this sender" hides every note from that uid in this browser only (localStorage `fbk-admin:hidden`, same as the old inbox). No delete.

  Everything is shown as plain text (never parsed as HTML). A test hook (`window.__admin`, which swaps Firestore for documents held in the page; sealed ones still need both codes) exists only when `navigator.webdriver` is set. **Who can see what:** the sealed parts of `chatlog` and `feedback` (message text, names, seats, reply details) need both codes. The rest is readable by anyone using the site's anonymous sign-in once these rules are in: which accounts wrote to the log and when, each feedback note's game, kind and done flag, the `who` documents (Online name, seat and analytics visitor ID per account), and, with the `pv` line, all page-view analytics, which can be joined with `who` to see roughly how long a named person played which game. Anyone signed in can also tick feedback done or not done. If someone should get the admin page, give them the door word, the number and the password; there's no per-person access.

- `e63334287a3deaa3/stats.js`: play time and personal bests for the Leaderboards (Back Room). Every active Back Room game loads it with one line in its `<head>`, `<script src="/e63334287a3deaa3/stats.js" data-game="<folder>" defer></script>`; it runs inside the game page, so it works the same when the game is opened directly or inside the home page, phone page or party layer. Play time: a second counts only while the tab is visible and there was a real key, click, touch, mouse movement or gamepad input in the last 2 minutes (made-up events and the still "mouse moves" browsers send when the page changes don't count), and at most 24 hours a day are counted for all games together. Bests come two ways. Some it reads from what the game already saves (`READ` in the file): Coil `coil:best` (longest snake), Shell Crackers `shellcr-rank` (Elo, once you've played a rated match), Updraft `updraft:best` (best height, fastest summit), Spire `spire:profile` (fastest full tower, towers climbed), Tumble Trials `tumble-stats` (crowns, shows), Showdown `sd:pstats` (biggest pot and hands played in practice) and Trivium `trivium.daily.*` and `trivium.streak` (best finished Daily score, best Daily streak). The rest come from one-line hooks at the moment it happens in the game, `window.BackRoomStats && BackRoomStats.add('kills', 1)`: Shell Crackers kills (practice and online, not the training range), Rumble Karts wrecks and rounds you top, Backrooms 1v1–4v4 kills and match wins (against the Entity and online), Boostball goals and match wins, Coil cut-offs, Odd One Out props found and rounds your team wins, Chromaflage hiders found and rounds you stay hidden, and Slipstream's fastest lap per track (`BackRoomStats.min('lap:<track id>', seconds)`, only for laps with no track-limits penalty). The hooks only record; they don't change the games. The API is `add(stat, n)` (a running count, at most 100 at a time), `min(stat, value)` (a time: lower wins) and `max(stat, value)`; stat names are short lowercase words (`a-z`, `0-9`, `:`, `-`), and a name starting with `lap:` or listed in `LOWER` is a time. Saved in the browser under localStorage `lbs:v1` (`{g: {<folder>: {s: seconds, <stat>: number}}, d: day, ds: seconds counted that day}`) and `lbs:uid` (the account it last published under). Published at most once a minute while something changed, and when the page closes, to the Firebase Realtime Database (same project and rules as the games) at `rooms/lb-all/players/<uid>` (prefix `lb`, not an invite prefix): the shared rules only let a browser write its own node, need `n, c, x, z, h, ts` (`x`/`z`/`h` always 0) and refuse names longer than 14 characters, so the node is `n` (the Online name `ol:name`, else the party name, else a game's saved name), `c` (colour), `ts` (server time), `v` (1), `st` (the seat tag from social.js, for convenience; readers work it out from the uid) and `g` (as above). The node is never removed on disconnect, because it is the player's entry. The first publish after each page load reads the node back and keeps the larger total or better best, so clearing the browser doesn't wipe the entry (and two devices on one account keep the higher of each, not the sum). Firebase: inside the home, phone or party layer it borrows that page's sign-in (`window.parent.Party`), so it's the same account the panels show; if the game is online it uses the game's own Firebase; otherwise, after a minute, it loads the SDK in a hidden same-origin frame and signs in there (the same saved sign-in, so the same uid), so it never touches the game's own Firebase globals (Trivium's are another project). **Everything is self-reported by the player's own browser**, so someone with developer tools can write any numbers into their own node; leaderboard.js checks and caps what it shows, but it isn't cheat-proof. To add a stat for a new game: load stats.js in the game with its folder name, then either add a reader to `READ` for something the game already saves or add a one-line hook where it happens, and give the game a row in `GAMES` in leaderboard.js. A test hook (`window.__lbs`) exists only when `navigator.webdriver` is set.
- `e63334287a3deaa3/leaderboard.js`: the Leaderboards (Back Room). A "Leaderboards" section under the games on the Back Room page and the phone page (one `<script defer>` in each page's `<head>`; the home page's `reveal()` also loads it, because the home page doesn't run the Back Room page's `<head>`). A Board menu picks "Hours (all games)" (rank, name · seat, total hours, the game played most) or a game, which shows that game's hours and its stat boards as buttons: Rumble Karts wrecks and round wins; Shell Crackers Elo and kills; Backrooms kills and match wins; Slipstream a fastest lap for every track someone has a time on ("Fastest lap — Monaco"); Boostball goals and match wins; Coil longest snake and cut-offs; Trivium best Daily score and streak; Showdown biggest pot and hands played (practice); Chromaflage hiders found and rounds stayed hidden; Spire fastest tower and towers climbed; Updraft best height and fastest summit; Odd One Out rounds won and props found; Tumble Trials crowns and shows. Each board shows the top 25 and, if you're further down, your own row after a gap; your row is highlighted with a "You" tag (you are the account party.js signs in with, or the one stats.js last published under). The choice of board is remembered in the browser (`lb:view`). It reads nothing until the section is near the screen (or a board is picked), then reads `rooms/lb-all/players` once; Refresh reads it again (at most every 8 seconds). It signs in through party.js (`window.Party`). Every node is untrusted and checked before it's shown: at most 3,000 nodes and 8,000 characters each; nodes from the future, from before the boards started (`EPOCH`, 21 October 2026) or not updated for a year are skipped; play time can't exceed 24 hours for every day since `EPOCH` (a node claiming more is skipped); running counts can't exceed a rate per second played in that game (`rate` in `GAMES`, e.g. half a kill a second), and each stat has a range (Elo 100–3000, Updraft height up to 1005 m and a summit no quicker than 2 minutes, a Spire tower no quicker than 20 s, a Slipstream lap no quicker than the track's length at 150 m/s, from `TRACKS`); unknown games, stats and tracks are ignored; names are cleaned, run through social.js's word filter and only ever set as text. To add a game, add a row to `GAMES` (folder, name and its stats: key, label, format, range and, for running counts, a rate); for a new Slipstream track, add it to `TRACKS`. A test hook (`window.__lb`) exists only when `navigator.webdriver` is set.
